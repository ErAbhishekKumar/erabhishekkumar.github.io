Program Elective II: Computational Fluid Dynamics [116702]

  • Unit 1.0: Fundamental Concepts
    • Introduction to Computational Fluid Dynamics: Scope and importance of CFD in aerospace and engineering applications
    • Relation between experimental
    • Analytical
    • And computational approaches
    • Fundamental assumptions: continuum hypothesis and Newtonian fluid model
    • Types of fluid flows: incompressible vs. compressible
    • Viscous vs. inviscid
    • Steady vs. unsteady
    • Basic Equations of Fluid Dynamics: Derivation and physical meaning of governing equations: continuity
    • Momentum
    • And energy
    • Integral and differential forms of conservation equations
    • Navier-Stokes equations and simplifying assumptions for laminar and inviscid flows
    • Role of viscosity
    • Pressure
    • And energy terms in governing flow behavior
    • Mathematical Classification of Fluid Flow Equations: Nature and classification of partial differential equations: elliptic
    • Parabolic
    • And hyperbolic
    • Physical interpretation of each PDE type and examples (Laplace
    • Diffusion
    • Wave equations)
    • Relevance of equation type to steady and unsteady flow problems
    • Mathematical behavior and numerical implications for each class
    • Well-Posed Problems and Boundary Conditions: Definition and importance of well-posedness: existence
    • Uniqueness
    • And stability of solutions
    • Classification of boundary conditions: Dirichlet
    • Neumann
    • And mixed
    • Selection of initial and boundary conditions for subsonic and supersonic flows
    • Impact of improper boundary specification on numerical convergence
    • Discretization of Partial Differential Equations: Concept and purpose of discretization in CFD
    • Finite difference approximations: forward
    • Backward
    • And central difference schemes
    • Taylor series expansion and truncation error analysis
    • Consistency
    • Accuracy
    • And convergence of discretized equations
    • Transformations and Computational Grids: Coordinate transformations: Cartesian
    • Curvilinear
    • And body-fitted coordinates
    • Structured and unstructured grid systems - advantages and limitations
    • Grid generation techniques and mesh quality criteria
    • Grid refinement and independence testing
    • Explicit Finite Difference Methods for Flow Problems: Time-dependent discretization: explicit time-marching methods
    • Application to model equations for subsonic
    • Supersonic
    • And viscous flows
    • Stability analysis: Courant-Friedrichs-Lewy (CFL) condition and von Neumann method
    • Limitations and practical implementation of explicit schemes
    • Numerical Stability
    • Convergence
    • And Accuracy: Relationship between stability
    • Consistency
    • And convergence (Lax equivalence theorem)
    • Sources of numerical errors: truncation
    • Round-off
    • And boundary approximations
    • Influence of grid resolution and time-step size on solution accuracy
    • Guidelines for achieving stable and convergent CFD simulations
  • Unit 2.0: Panel Methods
    • Introduction to Panel Methods: Concept of potential flow and its relevance to inviscid
    • Incompressible external aerodynamics
    • Simplification of flow field using Laplace's equation
    • Representation of body surfaces by distributed singularities (sources and vortices)
    • Importance of panel methods in aerodynamic analysis and design
    • Source Panel Method: Fundamentals of source singularity distribution along surface panels
    • Mathematical formulation of source strength and potential function
    • Enforcement of no-penetration boundary condition on non-lifting bodies
    • Computation of surface velocity and pressure coefficient for symmetric airfoils and bodies of revolution
    • Practical examples and limitations of the source panel approach
    • Vortex Panel Method: Need for vortex distribution to model circulation and lift in aerodynamic flows
    • Mathematical formulation of vortex panels and determination of vortex strength
    • Application of Kutta condition for trailing-edge flow
    • Calculation of lift
    • Pressure distribution
    • And streamline patterns for airfoils
    • Extension to combined source-vortex panel methods for more accurate modeling
    • Applications of Panel Methods: Use of panel methods for airfoil and wing performance prediction
    • Application to fuselage and external flow problems in aerospace and marine contexts
    • Comparison with finite difference / finite volume CFD methods in terms of computational cost and accuracy
    • Advantages and limitations in practical aerodynamic design and preliminary analysis
  • Unit 3.0: Discretization
    • Boundary Layer Equations and Methods of Solution: Derivation and physical interpretation of boundary layer equations from Navier-Stokes equations
    • Simplifying assumptions for laminar and turbulent boundary layers
    • Numerical approaches for boundary layer solution: finite difference and implicit schemes
    • Application to compressible and incompressible flows
    • Implicit Time-Dependent Methods for Inviscid and Viscous Flows: Concept of implicit discretization and its importance for stiff equations
    • Formulation of implicit schemes for inviscid and viscous compressible flows
    • Comparison between explicit and implicit time-marching methods
    • Advantages in terms of stability and convergence for high-speed flow computations
    • Concept of Numerical Dissipation: Definition and physical interpretation of numerical dissipation (artificial viscosity)
    • Role in damping oscillations and stabilizing high-speed flow simulations
    • Balancing accuracy and stability through controlled dissipation
    • Example of artificial viscosity terms in compressible flow solvers
    • Stability Properties of Explicit and Implicit Methods: Review of stability
    • Consistency
    • And convergence for time-dependent schemes
    • Von Neumann stability analysis and Courant-Friedrichs-Lewy (CFL) condition
    • Comparison of stability limits for explicit and implicit discretization methods
    • Practical criteria for selecting time steps and spatial resolution
    • Conservative Upwind Discretization for Hyperbolic Systems: Formulation of upwind differencing schemes for hyperbolic conservation laws
    • Concept of information propagation direction and characteristic speeds
    • Conservation form of finite difference equations for compressible flows
    • Role of flux vector splitting and Riemann solvers in upwind schemes
    • Advantages of Upwind Differencing Techniques: Comparison of upwind differencing with central differencing in terms of stability and accuracy
    • Reduction of numerical oscillations near shocks and discontinuities
    • Enhanced shock-capturing and monotonicity-preserving properties
    • Importance in modern CFD solvers for aerospace and turbomachinery flows
  • Unit 4.0: Finite Element Techniques
    • Introduction to Finite Element Techniques in CFD: Overview of finite element method (FEM) and its role in computational fluid dynamics
    • Comparison of FEM with finite difference and finite volume methods
    • Importance of FEM in solving complex geometries and boundary-dominated problems
    • Applications of FEM in incompressible and compressible flow simulations
    • Strong and Weak Formulations of Boundary Value Problems: Definition and derivation of strong formulation for differential equations
    • Introduction to weak formulation and its necessity in numerical analysis
    • Concept of test functions and weighting functions in weak form derivation
    • Physical interpretation of weak forms for fluid flow problems
    • Weighted Residual and Galerkin Formulations: General concept of weighted residual methods for approximate solutions
    • Types of weighting techniques: Collocation
    • Subdomain
    • Least Squares
    • And Galerkin
    • Derivation of the Galerkin formulation from the weighted residual approach
    • Application of the Galerkin method to ID convection-diffusion problems
    • Weak and Variational Formulations: Relationship between weak and variational formulations
    • Application of the principle of minimum potential energy
    • Role of boundary and initial conditions in variational methods
    • Advantages of variational formulations in ensuring solution smoothness and convergence
    • Piecewise-Defined Shape Functions: Concept of domain discretization into elements
    • Construction of shape (interpolation) functions for linear and quadratic elements
    • Properties of shape functions: continuity
    • Completeness
    • And compatibility
    • Evaluation of element matrices and assembly of the global system
    • Implementation of the Finite Element Method (FEM): Step-by-step procedure for FEM implementation in CFD: Discretization of domain
    • Selection of shape functions
    • Formulation of element equations
    • Assembly of global equations
    • Application of boundary conditions
    • Integration over elements using Gaussian quadrature
    • Numerical examples and discussion of computational efficiency
    • The Solution Procedure and Post-Processing: Matrix formation and solution of algebraic equations using direct and iterative methods
    • Handling of symmetric and sparse matrices
    • Post-processing: velocity
    • Pressure
    • And temperature field reconstruction
    • Accuracy
    • Convergence
    • And verification of FEM solutions
  • Unit 5.0: Finite Volume Formulations I
    • Introduction to Finite Volume Techniques: Concept and principle of conservation over a finite control volume
    • Derivation of finite volume formulations from the integral form of governing equations
    • Comparison of finite volume
    • Finite difference
    • And finite element methods
    • Applications of finite volume approach in compressible and incompressible flow solvers
    • Cell-Centered Formulation: Structure of control volumes around cell centers
    • Evaluation of fluxes through cell faces and formulation of discrete conservation equations
    • Role of cell-centered storage in structured and unstructured grids
    • Implementation aspects in compressible flow solvers
    • Time-Stepping Techniques: Lax-Wendroff and Runge-Kutta Methods: Lax-Wendroff scheme: formulation
    • Second-order accuracy
    • And stability aspects
    • Runge-Kutta time-stepping: multi-stage explicit schemes for time advancement
    • Comparison of Lax-Wendroff and Runge-Kutta schemes in terms of accuracy and computational cost
    • Applications in unsteady flow and shock-capturing problems
    • Multi-Stage Time-Stepping and Accuracy Considerations: Concept of multi-stage integration for improving temporal accuracy
    • Stability limits and convergence behavior of multi-stage methods
    • Order of accuracy and truncation error estimation
    • Practical guidelines for selecting time-step size and scheme parameters
    • Cell-Vertex Formulation: Difference between cell-centered and cell-vertex storage approaches
    • Advantages of vertex-based formulations for gradient evaluation and flux interpolation
    • Applications in finite volume schemes for unstructured grids
    • Use in aerofoil and curved surface flow simulations
  • Unit 6.0: Finite Volume Formulations II
    • FDM-like Finite Volume Techniques: Relationship between finite difference and finite volume formulations
    • Derivation of FDM-like schemes preserving flux conservation and numerical stability
    • Hybrid methods combining the benefits of both formulations
    • Examples of simple FDM-like discretizations for diffusion and advection equations
    • Central and Upwind Type Discretization: Concept of information propagation direction in flow fields
    • Central differencing: accuracy
    • Simplicity
    • And limitations
    • Upwind differencing: stability and robustness in convective-dominated problems
    • Flux vector splitting and Roe's scheme concepts
    • Treatment of Derivatives in Finite Volume Methods: Gradient and divergence approximations within a control volume
    • Use of Green-Gauss and least squares methods for derivative evaluation
    • Ensuring conservation and consistency across cell interfaces
    • Impact of derivative accuracy on solution stability and convergence
